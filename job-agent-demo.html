1Input: the job posting

A sample posting I wrote for testing (not a real company). The resume is my real one, available on the Resume page.

QA Engineer, Mobile Apps — Example Corp (San Diego, hybrid) We're looking for a QA Engineer to test our Android and iOS apps. Responsibilities: write and execute test cases; perform functional, regression and boundary testing; file clear bug reports in JIRA; maintain test plans in TestRail; work with developers to reproduce issues. Requirements: 2+ years of software QA experience; mobile app testing; JIRA and TestRail; strong written communication. Nice to have: ISTQB certification, Python or Appium automation, Linux.

2Fit score: 9/10 7.9 s

Matches

  • 2+ years of software QA experience: Seth has over four years of cumulative QA experience across Qualcomm Technologies and Stanford University.
  • Mobile app testing: Seth has hands-on experience in mobile device app testing and has worked on Android app testing, including reverse engineering and compatibility patching.
  • JIRA and TestRail: Seth has experience using JIRA and TestRail, as evidenced by his work at Qualcomm Technologies and Stanford University, where he created test cases and maintained documentation.
  • Strong written communication: Seth has experience in technical documentation, defect reporting, and creating test plans and project requirement templates, which demonstrate strong written communication skills.

Gaps

  • The job description specifies '2+ years of software QA experience,' and while Seth has over four years of experience, it's not explicitly stated in his resume that all of this experience is in QA roles. Some of his experience is listed as 'Administrative Assistant' and 'Social Media and Marketing Professional,' which may not count toward QA experience.
  • Python or Appium automation: Seth does not mention experience with Python or Appium automation, which are listed as 'nice to have' in the job description.

My review: the score is reasonable, but the third match says JIRA and TestRail are evidenced by my work at Qualcomm and Stanford. They're only in my skills list. The first gap is also off: the resume does describe four years of QA work. Same blind spot as the fact-check below.

What this run shows

  • The fact-check helps but isn't enough on its own. On this posting I recorded 5 runs; the check flagged something in 1 of them. In the two runs I reviewed line by line, the final letter still contained at least one unsupported claim.
  • Its blind spot is consistent: a tool listed under Skills gets attached to a specific job. A bigger model didn't fix it, so the planned fix is structural: split the resume into sections in code and check each claim against only its matching section.
  • That's why the workflow ends with human review. The tool drafts and screens; I verify before anything is sent.
